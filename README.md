# Beyond the Balance Sheet: Analysing Effective Tax Rate Volatility and Deferred Tax Recognition at Tata Motors

## Project Overview

This project analyses Tata Motors Group’s consolidated financial disclosures across FY2022–23, FY2023–24, and FY2024–25 to understand changes in the **effective tax rate (ETR)** and the role of current and deferred tax expense in reported tax outcomes.

The analysis combines financial statement interpretation, Excel-based calculations, source validation, and reconciliation checks to examine how deferred tax movements can affect the relationship between profit before tax and reported income tax expense.

## Objectives

- Calculate and compare effective tax rates across financial years.
- Distinguish current tax expense from deferred tax expense or credit.
- Examine the impact of deferred tax recognition on reported tax outcomes.
- Compare reported tax expense with cash income tax paid, where disclosed.
- Validate extracted financial figures against source disclosures.
- Document reconciliation differences and unresolved questions transparently.

## Key Findings

- **FY2023–24:** Continuing operations reported a tax credit of ₹3,851.64 crore, including a deferred tax credit of ₹8,788.77 crore. The reported effective tax rate was approximately **−13.8%**.
- **FY2024–25:** Continuing operations reported an effective tax rate of approximately **31.1%**. On a total basis that includes discontinued operations, the effective tax rate was approximately **27.4%**.
- **Deferred tax recognition:** The analysis highlights how deferred tax movements can materially influence reported tax expense and effective tax rates.
- **Cash tax versus accounting tax:** Cash income tax paid is examined separately from income tax expense, as the two measures represent different aspects of taxation.
- **Reconciliation:** A ₹1,669 crore difference between two FY2024–25 deferred-tax figures remains unresolved and is documented for further investigation.

These findings illustrate why reported effective tax rates should be interpreted alongside the underlying financial statement disclosures and accounting context.

## Methodology

1. Extract relevant financial figures from Tata Motors’ published financial disclosures.
2. Separate profit before tax, current tax expense, deferred tax expense or credit, and total tax expense where available.
3. Calculate effective tax rate as:

   `Effective Tax Rate = Income Tax Expense / Profit Before Tax × 100`

4. Compare annual results and investigate material changes.
5. Perform formula checks, cross-source validation, and reconciliation checks in Excel.
6. Record discrepancies, source limitations, and items requiring further verification.

## Tools Used

- **Microsoft Excel** — financial calculations, analysis, validation checks, and reconciliation
- **Financial statement analysis** — effective tax rate and deferred tax interpretation
- **Source documentation** — traceability of reported figures and unresolved differences

## Validation and Limitations

The workbook includes 10 automated tests and 13 additional validation checks. The checks did not identify formula or tie-out failures in the tested items; however, passing checks do not mean every source discrepancy has been resolved.

The ₹1,669 crore deferred-tax difference remains an open reconciliation item. Certain detailed FY2024–25 deferred-tax inputs also require further verification against the relevant annual-report notes.

The FY2023–24 comparative figures and reported effective tax rates should be interpreted in the context of the specific financial statement basis used. This project does not claim to resolve every difference across disclosures or establish the sustainability of future tax rates.
