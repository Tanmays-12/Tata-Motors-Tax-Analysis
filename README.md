# Beyond the Balance Sheet: Analysing Effective Tax Rate Volatility and Deferred Tax Recognition at Tata Motors

*A financial and tax analysis of Tata Motors Group's effective tax rate (ETR), current and deferred tax movements, and tax-related disclosures across FY2022–23, FY2023–24, and FY2024–25.*

## 1. Project Overview

This project examines how Tata Motors Group's reported tax expense and effective tax rate changed over three financial years. It focuses on the relationship between profit before tax, current tax, deferred tax, and cash taxes paid, alongside the disclosures that help explain significant movements.

The analysis highlights why reported tax expense cannot always be interpreted in isolation and why deferred tax recognition, tax credits, and the distinction between accounting tax expense and cash tax payments matter when assessing a company's financial statements.

The project combines financial statement analysis with structured reconciliations, consistency checks, and documented limitations to distinguish supported findings from items that require further verification.

## 2. Objectives

- Analyse effective tax rate movements across FY2022–23 to FY2024–25.
- Examine changes in current tax expense and deferred tax expense or credit.
- Understand the contribution of deferred tax movements to reported tax results.
- Compare accounting tax expense with reported cash income taxes paid.
- Review relevant disclosures relating to deferred tax assets and unused tax losses.
- Validate calculations against available reported figures and document unresolved discrepancies.
- Present findings in a structured format suitable for financial analysis and professional review.

## 3. Key Findings

### Effective Tax Rate Volatility

The reported effective tax rate changed materially over the three-year period:

| Financial Year | Effective Tax Rate |
|---|---:|
| FY2022–23 | 20.7% |
| FY2023–24 | −13.8% |
| FY2024–25 | 27.4% |

FY2023–24 recorded a tax credit despite a positive profit-before-tax figure on the basis used in the analysis. FY2024–25 returned to a positive tax expense, with a continuing-operations ETR of 31.1%.

These figures illustrate why the ETR should be interpreted alongside the underlying tax expense, profit measure, and relevant disclosures. Different profit-before-tax bases can produce different ETR calculations.

### Current and Deferred Tax Movements

Amounts in ₹ crore.

| Tax Component | FY2022–23 | FY2023–24 | FY2024–25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current tax expense | 3,258 | 4,937 | 5,023 |
| Deferred tax expense / (credit) | (2,554) | (8,789) | 5,479 |

Deferred tax movements were a major contributor to the volatility in reported tax results, particularly in FY2023–24, when a substantial deferred tax credit reduced the overall tax expense.

In FY2024–25, deferred tax shifted back to an expense. The movement warrants interpretation alongside the company's disclosures and should not, by itself, be treated as proof of a particular cause.

### Deferred Tax Assets and Unused Losses

The analysis reviews disclosures concerning the recognition of deferred tax assets associated with unused losses, including the disclosed recognition by Jaguar Land Rover (JLR).

The recognition of deferred tax assets is relevant because it can materially affect reported tax expense. However, the accounting recognition of a deferred tax asset is distinct from the timing and amount of any future cash tax benefit.

### Accounting Tax Expense vs. Cash Taxes Paid

Reported cash income taxes paid differed from the reported accounting tax expense.

Amounts in ₹ crore.

| Metric | FY2023–24 | FY2024–25 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash income tax paid, net | 4,516 | 3,991 |
| Reported tax expense | — | 10,606 |

The difference highlights the importance of distinguishing cash tax payments from tax expense recognised in the income statement. The two measures reflect different accounting and payment timings and should not be treated as interchangeable.

*Note: Figures are presented in ₹ crore unless stated otherwise. ETR calculations depend on the profit-before-tax basis used. Refer to the detailed case study for calculation bases, source references, and limitations.*

## 4. Methodology

The analysis followed a structured financial-review process:

1. **Source review:** Examine available annual-report disclosures and reported financial figures relevant to tax expense, deferred tax, and cash taxes.
2. **Data organisation:** Structure the extracted figures by financial year and tax category.
3. **ETR analysis:** Compare reported ETRs and review the relationship between tax expense and profit before tax.
4. **Tax reconciliation:** Examine current tax and deferred tax movements and their contribution to total tax expense or credit.
5. **Cross-checking:** Compare selected figures and calculations with available reported totals.
6. **Validation:** Apply formula checks, tie-outs, and additional consistency checks to identify discrepancies.
7. **Documentation:** Record assumptions, limitations, unresolved issues, and areas requiring further source verification.

The analysis is intended as a financial-statement review, not as a formal tax opinion or independent audit.

## 6. Validation and Limitations

Validation was an important part of the project. The workbook includes 10 automated tests and 13 additional validation checks. The tested items recorded no formula or tie-out failures, but this does **not** mean every source figure or interpretation has been independently confirmed.

The following limitations are documented:

- An FY2022–23 profit-before-tax discrepancy remains relevant to the ETR analysis. The numerical difference matches the share of profit from joint ventures and associates, but the reason for the presentation difference has not been conclusively confirmed.
- Different profit-before-tax bases produce different ETR results. The selected ETR should therefore be interpreted with its calculation basis.
- Certain FY2024–25 note-level deferred-tax reconciliation inputs have not been independently verified against the complete annual-report disclosures.
- A ₹1,669 crore difference between the note-level net deferred tax asset and the balance-sheet figure remains unresolved in the analysis.
- A reconciliation result should not be treated as proof of an accounting explanation unless that explanation is supported by the relevant source disclosure.

These limitations are retained to make the analysis transparent and to distinguish validated calculations from unresolved questions.
