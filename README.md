# Beyond the Balance Sheet: Analysing Effective Tax Rate Volatility and Deferred Tax Recognition at Tata Motors

## Project Overview

This project analyses Tata Motors Group’s consolidated financial disclosures across FY2022–23, FY2023–24, and FY2024–25 to examine effective tax rate (ETR) volatility and the role of current and deferred tax in reported financial results.

The project combines financial statement analysis, Excel-based calculations, source validation, and reconciliation checks to understand how deferred tax movements can influence the relationship between profit before tax and reported income tax expense.

## Objectives

- Calculate and compare effective tax rates across financial years.
- Distinguish current tax expense from deferred tax expense or credit.
- Examine the impact of deferred tax recognition on reported tax outcomes.
- Compare reported tax expense with cash income tax paid, where disclosed.
- Validate extracted financial figures against source disclosures.
- Document discrepancies and unresolved reconciliation items transparently.

## Key Findings

### 1. FY2023–24: Deferred Tax and Reported Tax Credit

Continuing operations reported a tax credit of ₹3,851.64 crore, including a deferred tax credit of ₹8,788.77 crore. The reported effective tax rate was approximately **−13.8%**.

This illustrates how deferred tax movements can materially affect reported tax expense and the resulting effective tax rate.

### 2. FY2024–25: Effective Tax Rate Analysis

For FY2024–25, the calculated effective tax rate was approximately:

- **31.1%** for continuing operations.
- **27.4%** on a total basis including discontinued operations.

The comparison highlights the importance of defining the financial statement basis consistently when calculating and interpreting effective tax rates.

### 3. Cash Tax Versus Accounting Tax

Cash income tax paid and reported income tax expense are analysed as separate measures. Their differences matter when interpreting tax-related financial disclosures because cash payments and accounting expenses do not necessarily correspond to the same period.

### 4. Deferred-Tax Reconciliation

A ₹1,669 crore difference between two FY2024–25 deferred-tax figures remains unresolved in the analysis. The discrepancy is documented as an open reconciliation item rather than being assigned an unverified explanation.

## Methodology

The project follows a structured financial analysis and validation process:

1. Extract relevant figures from published financial disclosures.
2. Identify profit before tax, current tax expense, deferred tax expense or credit, and total tax expense where available.
3. Calculate effective tax rates using the relevant profit and tax figures.
4. Compare results across financial years and investigate material movements.
5. Use Excel to perform calculations, formula checks, cross-source comparisons, and reconciliation checks.
6. Document discrepancies, verification limitations, and outstanding questions.

### Effective Tax Rate Formula

**Effective Tax Rate (ETR) = Income Tax Expense ÷ Profit Before Tax × 100**

The calculation must use a consistent reporting basis for both income tax expense and profit before tax.

## Validation and Quality Checks

The Excel workbook includes:

- 10 automated tests
- 13 additional validation checks
- Documented source and reconciliation issues for further investigation

The checks did not identify formula or tie-out failures in the items tested. However, passing validation checks does not establish that every extracted figure is independently verified or that every discrepancy has been resolved.

The ₹1,669 crore deferred-tax difference remains open. Certain detailed FY2024–25 deferred-tax inputs require further verification against the relevant annual-report notes.

The analysis also recognises that comparative financial figures and reported effective tax rates may vary depending on the reporting basis used. Unresolved differences are documented rather than presented as confirmed explanations.

## Limitations

- The analysis relies on published financial disclosures and the availability of comparable reported figures.
- Certain detailed deferred-tax inputs require further source-level verification.
- The ₹1,669 crore reconciliation difference remains unresolved.
- Effective tax rates can be influenced by deferred tax recognition, changes in the composition of reported operations, and other accounting factors.
- The results are historical and should not be interpreted as forecasts of future tax rates.
